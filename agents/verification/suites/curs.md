# `curs` -- `test/assert-curs.py`, the gestures a player makes

**The sixteenth suite, and the one every "only the TRIGGER is unverifiable headlessly" sentence in this file now points at.** It is the second with no `--create` phase, and its reason is `mig21`'s turned round: those worlds cannot be REBUILT by the binary on this machine, and this one cannot be built by a MAP GENERATOR at all, on any binary. A `game.players` entry exists only where somebody once connected and nothing a script can do makes one, so phase one is a save a graphical CLIENT made -- `test/fixtures-player/`, cut down to one player on nine chunks and committed. This mod builds its own rigs into it at `fk_on_init`, which fires because the fixture has never seen this mod, and then drives seven gestures through `player.build_from_cursor` and `player.mine_entity`.

**THE PLAYER IS IN THE GOD CONTROLLER, AND THAT IS A MEASUREMENT RATHER THAN A SHORTCUT.** The only headless route to a save file is `--start-server` and `game.server_save` -- `--benchmark` never writes one -- and a server load DISCONNECTS every player and DESTROYS their character. A disconnected player cannot be given one back: `player.character = <a character just created>` is refused by name, *"User isn't connected; can't write character"*, and `set_controller{type = character, character = ...}` returns cleanly, leaves `player.character` nil, and `build_from_cursor` after it raises `on_pre_build` and places nothing. God is the controller such a player CAN hold, and what it costs was measured against the connected character it stands in for, on one gesture -- a part fast-replaced onto a loaded belt -- with every column identical:

| | character, connected | god, disconnected |
|---|---|---|
| `on_pre_build` / `on_pre_player_mined_item` | yes | yes |
| `on_player_mined_entity`'s buffer | `iron-plate x6, transport-belt x1` | identical |
| `on_player_mined_item` | twice | twice |
| `on_built_entity` | `bbb-balancer-part` | identical |
| `player_index` on all five | 1 | 1 |
| belts / plates / cursor | 7->8, 87->93, 5->4->5 | identical |
| the mod's four lines | formed, refused, dissolved, handed back | identical |

The one thing god mode changes is `build_distance`, which is unbounded there and 10 for a character. The observer TELEPORTS the player next to every target anyway: the reach is not what is under test, and a rig that only worked from across the map would be one a person could not reproduce.

**AND SINCE 2026-09-15 TWO BANDS ABOUT THE CURVED EXIT**, which are the mod portal report "Belt corners on exit" made as gestures and the linked-belt probe's red proof. Both rigs are a DEAD-ENDED 2->2 over four parts with a spare EDGELESS row above it whose north faces are the gesture row, which is the `bmin` idiom and is forced: under the one-belt-per-part rule the four working parts have no free face between them. They report on a `curve tag=` line of their own rather than through `sample`, because (h3) spills on purpose:

| gesture | what came out |
|---|---|
| **(h1)** one belt clicked onto a free face | **grabbed**: `compiled cluster 157 2->3 over 4 ports, 27 entities`, the engine reading the belt `right`, an interface on the part below it, and **7 plates on the player's line** 130 ticks later |
| **(h2)** a line laid from three tiles out, one belt per click across BOTH free faces | **never grabbed**: no interface at any of eight samples, **0 items** on the line, and not one compile in the window |

**The engine control is band (i)'s anti-vacuity and it is doing real work**: without it a rig that was simply not curve-eligible would satisfy every `false` above. The 127 items are the third band's own: releasing a port halves the butterfly (P 4 -> 2) on a machine that has been full for 130 ticks, so what will not fit reaches the ground. **That is the one window in this suite where the mod may spill**, named by line index the way `edge` names its four rather than the check being weakened.

**RED-PROVEN by putting `beltTypes` back on `findAnyByPos`**, which is the probe's own six-type list: **three assertions, all of them band (i)'s, and every band (h) number byte-identical to the green run** -- `band (i): the face belt was grabbed at lb-2 ... with a linked belt OUTPUT end feeding its rear`, the same at `lb-10`, and `band (i) recompiled the balancer: [('163', '2', '3', '4', '27')]`. The grabbed belt reads `shape=straight` in that run, which is the defect in one word: an interface under a belt the engine will not curve is a side-load.

**AND SINCE 2026-09-20 BAND (j), AN UNDERGROUND PAIR TURNED FROM ITS FAR END**, which is Gamer433's third report on the same portal thread: the balancer's arrows did not follow an underground belt rotated at the half AWAY from the balancer. The engine swaps both halves of a pair when either is rotated and raises `on_player_rotated_entity` for the one under the cursor alone, so the half beside the part changed from input to output with nothing inside the two-tile gate reported. `farUndergroundTurned` (`guest/go/main.go`) is the fix and it is POSITIONAL: the partner attribute is `neighbours`, a union, at the 2.0.77 pin and `underground_belt_neighbour` at 2.1.17, and one tree ships on both, so every tile on the rotated half's own axis within its prototype's `max_underground_distance` is offered to the ordinary neighbourhood walk. It over-approximates, and what that costs is a classification the fingerprint throws away. One `type_is` for a rotated entity that is not an underground, three host calls for one that is, per keypress and never per tick; the mod binds 69 members where it bound 68.

The rig is a two-part row with an ordinary input on its west part and an underground pair running WEST beside its east part, far half at x=5: two inputs, no output, no network. The player rotates the far half. Measured on 2.0.77 on the `release/2.0` arm: the near half reads `near-output=true` before and `false` after, `compiled cluster ... 1->1 over 1 ports` lands before any audit, the east part carries an interface two ticks later, the audit is `(12, 169, 12, 1, 0, 1)`, and 106 items reach the chest. **Red-proven by making the call unreachable**: exactly three assertions, `post-urot` at `(12, 169, 11, 1, 1, 1)`, no interface at `urot-2`, and `compiled []` -- and the chest still takes 104, because the audit marker two ticks later repairs what the event path missed, which is why the sample and the compile window both close BEFORE it. The band costs the suite +1 cluster and +2 parts on every audit tuple and one network from `post-urot` on. All seventeen suites are green in both `-gc` arms on that worktree with the fix in, one invocation each, and the leaking arm's slopes are unmoved at **1,280 / 176 / 1,209 / 16 / 560 / 3,736 / 2,080 / 192 B per primitive** over 3.91 MiB, the collected arm at 0 forward-progress deadlines. **AND ON TRUNK, 2026-09-20, Factorio 2.1.17 (build 87315)**: `make test`, `make GC=leaking test` and `make datastage-check` all exit 0 at the fix commit, `flip` printing its SKIP, band (j) reading the same `(12, 169, 12, 1, 0, 1)`, `1->1` and 106 items in both arms, the leaking slopes the same eight figures over 3.91 MiB, the collected arm at 0 deadlines, and the dump gate at 32 `ok` lines with no golden moved. The `release/2.0` recut at 0.2.6 is owed.

**NOTHING REACHES THE GROUND IN THE WHOLE RUN OUTSIDE THAT ONE WINDOW, and that is the headline rather than a formality.** Every removal here was made by a player, so everything a machine could not take back is offered to that player first -- where the `edge` suite's twin of the `bmin` leg puts 124 of the same 128 items on the floor, because a headless `--create` has nobody to offer them to. `assert-curs.py` fails on a non-zero `ground=` at any sample.

**THE FULL-INVENTORY NEGATIVE NEEDED TWO THINGS AND THE FIRST RUN HAD ONLY ONE.** Factorio REFILLS AN EMPTIED CURSOR FROM THE MAIN INVENTORY: place the last belt from a cursor while the inventory holds more, and the engine moves them into the cursor -- which FREES the slot they were in and gives the hand-back somewhere to land. Measured: main went two belts to zero across the click and the refused piece came back into main anyway, on an inventory that was otherwise full. The order is empty, hold ONE, fill, click. And `is_full` is not the signal either: an inventory whose eighty slots were all occupied answered false, because a partly-filled stack still has room in it. What the gesture needs is that one more steel chest cannot be inserted, and that is what the observer reports.

**THE RED PROOF IS THE RUN THIS SUITE WAS WRITTEN AGAINST.** Gesture (a) is the belt a refused fast replace destroyed, and the suite was built and run BEFORE the fix. On the unfixed guest the assertion script fails on exactly seven lines and nothing else -- no restore line, `restore-1`, `restore-2` and `restore-8` all reading `tile=empty`, and the player one belt richer than they started at all three -- while every other gesture above is green to the number. With the fix the same log reads `restored=1` and `tile=express-transport-belt dir=12 items=0`, and the belt count does not move.

**What it does NOT cover is the pixels.** The flying text's position and its colour, the build preview over the cursor and the `utility/cannot_build` sound are a human's still, and [`test/interactive/README.md`](../../../test/interactive/README.md) is where they live. That checklist is now about how the gestures LOOK rather than about whether they work.
